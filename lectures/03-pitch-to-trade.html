What exactly are we trading?

CLP / USDCLP

CLP is the Chilean peso. USDCLP means pesos per US dollar: USDCLP falling means the peso strengthens.

Copper

Commodity futures reflect the market price of copper; futures are contracts, not shares of a company.

Pair / relative value

A long CLP + short copper trade bets on their relative performance, not simply on a stronger economy.

LA TOUR · CASE STUDY / 05

Turn prices into comparable returns

Price levels in different currencies cannot be subtracted meaningfully. Use returns, oriented consistently.

import numpy as np
clp_usd = 1.0 / df["usdclp"]
clp_ret = np.log(clp_usd).diff()
cu_ret  = np.log(df["copper_settle"]).diff()

For beginners: log return ≈ percentage change for small moves. A 1% CLP appreciation gives about +0.01; 1% copper appreciation gives about +0.01.

LA TOUR · CASE STUDY / 06

What does beta actually mean?

Estimate the relationship on a past-only training window:

rCLP,t = α + β rCu,t + εt

Here β measures the historical sensitivity of CLP returns to copper returns. The pitch quotes β ≈ 0.15 (weekly regression). That figure is a pitch claim to reproduce, not an independently verified estimate.

β = 0.15 (example)

A +1% weekly copper return is associated with about +0.15% CLP return, on average in the estimated sample.

Not an order size

A regression coefficient is not by itself a futures contract ratio. Contract multiplier, FX notional, volatility and funding matter.

LA TOUR · CASE STUDY / 07

Compute a dislocation score

A simplified research residual is gₜ = R₍CLP,6m₎ − βₜ R₍Cu,6m₎. Standardize relative to the training-window distribution:

zₜ = (gₜ − μtrain) / σtrain

The pitch reports approximately z = −1.4. A negative value means the peso underperformed relative to this model. It does not prove the gap will close.

How do we size the two legs?

The pitch gives a β-hedged expression: long CLP / short copper. Actual implementation requires an explicit translation into dollar notionals and contract counts.

# Conceptual; NOT broker-ready code
clp_usd_notional = 100_000   # illustrative
copper_usd_exposure = beta * clp_usd_notional
# Compute futures contracts only after accounting for
# copper price, contract multiplier, and FX convention.

A short copper position may offset a copper-sensitive component of CLP returns, but FX and futures risk are not completely hedged. Margin, carry, liquidity and gap risk remain.

Backtest: test a rule, not a chart

Every daily backtest decision must use information known before the return being scored.

signal = (zscore <= -1.5).astype(int)
position = signal.shift(1).fillna(0)
# Toy return decomposition: use correctly sized leg returns
gross = position * (clp_ret - hedge_weight * cu_ret)
turnover = position.diff().abs().fillna(position.abs())
net = gross - turnover * cost_per_turn

This is an explanatory simplified backtest. Real CLP funding, copper futures rolls, margin and instrument execution timing require additional modeling.

What would convince us?

Evidence FOR

Positive chronological out-of-sample performance after costs; stable signal across regimes; sensible economics; realizable instruments.

Evidence AGAINST

The score does not forecast convergence; only works under tuned lookbacks; disappears after funding/roll costs; dominated by DXY or rates risk.

Compare with

No-trade, long-CLP alone, short-copper alone, and simpler dollar/rates-conditioned alternatives.

Ask about

Maximum drawdown, worst period, turnover, exposure concentration, crash behavior and data leakage.
